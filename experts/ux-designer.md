# Expert knowledge: UX/UI Designer

Source knowledge to distill into workflow subagents. Not an agent itself.

## Product thinking and flows

- Jobs to Be Done applied to interaction design: design the flow around the job the user is hiring the product for, not around the feature list
- User flows and task flows: happy path plus the error, empty, loading, and edge states that most designs forget
- Information architecture: navigation models, content hierarchy, progressive disclosure
- Reducing steps-to-value and time-to-first-value; every extra screen or field is friction that costs conversion and retention

## Usability and interaction

- Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics as a working checklist, not a poster
- Fitts's Law, Hick's Law, and the practical cost of choice overload
- Forgiving design: sensible defaults, undo over confirm, inline validation over post-submit errors
- The four UI states every screen must design for: ideal/populated, empty, error, and loading/partial

## Visual and UI craft

- Layout systems: spacing scales, grids, visual hierarchy, typographic rhythm
- Design tokens and a component system over one-off screens, so the UI stays consistent and buildable
- Responsive and mobile-first thinking; touch targets and thumb zones
- Motion with purpose (feedback, continuity), never decoration that slows the task

## Accessibility (a requirement, not polish)

- WCAG 2.2 AA as the baseline: color contrast, focus order, keyboard operability, semantic structure, labels and alt text
- Designing for screen readers and reduced-motion/assistive settings from the start
- Accessibility overlaps with SEO and general robustness - it is rarely a pure cost

## Conversion and landing pages

- Landing page anatomy: above-the-fold value proposition, proof, objection handling, single primary CTA, friction removal
- Message-match between the ad/entry point and the landing headline
- The one-primary-action rule per screen; competing CTAs dilute conversion
- Trust and credibility signals (social proof, guarantees, specificity over hype)

## Working with product and business

- Balancing the ideal UX against what is most profitable and buildable now - the tension is the point, not a failure
- Wireframe and low-fidelity first to settle structure before visual detail
- Designing measurable flows: instrument the funnel so the design can be validated, not just admired
- Collaborating with engineering on feasibility and with marketing on positioning without surrendering usability

## AI-era design (2026)

- Designing AI-native interactions: prompt/response surfaces, streaming states, confidence and citation display, graceful failure and correction
- Setting user expectations for non-deterministic output; making it easy to steer, undo, and verify
- Trust, transparency, and consent as first-class UX concerns when AI is at the core of the value proposition
