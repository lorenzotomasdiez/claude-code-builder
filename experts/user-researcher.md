# Expert knowledge: User Researcher

Source knowledge to distill into workflow subagents. Not an agent itself.

Distinct from `researcher.md` (general/desk research) and from `product-owner.md` (who decides what to build). This role owns the evidence about actual humans: who they are, what they are trying to get done, and whether the demand being assumed is real.

## Discovery practice

- Continuous Discovery Habits (Teresa Torres): weekly touchpoints with users, opportunity solution trees, assumption mapping over one-off research projects
- Jobs to Be Done (Moesta, Ulwick): interview for the switch - the struggling moment, the trigger, what they hired and fired, the anxieties and habits that hold them back
- The Mom Test (Rob Fitzpatrick): ask about their past behavior and life, never about your idea; "would you use this?" produces nothing usable
- Distinguish the buyer, the user, and the champion - in B2B and client work they are usually three different people with conflicting incentives
- Demand-side evidence beats stated preference: what did they already try, hack together, pay for, or abandon?

## Methods and when each applies

- Generative/qualitative (interviews, contextual inquiry, diary studies): use when you do not yet know what the problem is - 5 to 8 well-chosen participants surfaces most of what one segment will tell you
- Evaluative (usability testing, concept tests, prototype walkthroughs): use when you have a candidate solution and need to know if it works
- Quantitative (surveys, analytics, funnel and cohort analysis): use to size and confirm something qualitative research already surfaced, never to discover it
- Fake-door, landing-page, and concierge tests: use to measure willingness to act before anything is built
- Competitive and workaround analysis: spreadsheets, WhatsApp groups, and manual processes are the most honest evidence that a job exists and is unserved

## Evidence quality

- Grade every claim: observed behavior > reported behavior > stated intention > assumption > analogy from another market
- Label assumptions as assumptions, explicitly, rather than laundering them into findings
- Sample and survivorship bias: the users who are easiest to reach are usually the least representative of the ones you are missing
- Leading questions, social desirability bias, and the tendency of participants to be kind about a maker's idea
- Small-n qualitative findings are directional, not statistical - report them as such, with confidence stated
- One vivid anecdote is not a pattern; three independent accounts of the same struggle is the start of one

## Turning research into product input

- Personas only when grounded in real segments and real behavior; otherwise use jobs and scenarios, which are harder to fake
- Journey maps that include the emotional low points and the moments where users currently give up
- Opportunity framing: state the problem as the user's unmet need, never as a missing feature
- Separate the problem's severity, frequency, and reach - a severe problem that happens once a year is not the same bet as a mild one that happens daily
- Name the riskiest assumption in the whole idea and what evidence would settle it

## The client-work failure mode

- The client is a stakeholder, not a user, unless they are also the user - their confidence about user needs is a hypothesis, not data
- "Our customers keep asking for X" usually means one loud customer asked for X once; ask who, when, and what they did next
- Requirements arriving as a solution ("we need a dashboard") hide the underlying job - work backwards to the decision the dashboard was supposed to support
- When no user access is available, say so plainly and mark every user claim as unvalidated rather than proceeding as if it were known

## Ethics

- Informed consent, the right to withdraw, and being honest about what the research is for
- Privacy and data minimization when recording or storing sessions (GDPR and regional equivalents)
- Compensating participants fairly, and not extracting free consulting from users under the label of research
