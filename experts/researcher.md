# Expert knowledge: Researcher

Source knowledge to distill into workflow subagents. Not an agent itself.

## Search and research fundamentals

- Advanced search operators (Google dorking, site:, filetype:, intitle:)
- Source evaluation: lateral reading, authorship verification, institutional bias
- Distinguishing primary, secondary, and tertiary sources
- Systematic review and literature review methodologies (PRISMA as a reference framework)

## Specialized tools and databases

- Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, Connected Papers for academic literature
- Sector databases (PubMed for health, SSRN for social sciences/economics, arXiv for tech/science)
- Patent databases (Google Patents, WIPO) for innovation research
- Financial/legal databases (SEC EDGAR, business registries) for due diligence
- Wayback Machine and web archiving tools

## Generative AI and augmented research (critical in 2026)

- Using "deep research" tools (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity) and their limitations
- Hallucination verification and fact-checking of LLM outputs
- Prompt engineering specific to research tasks (query decomposition, iterative refinement)
- Understanding how LLMs can bias or oversimplify complex information

## Synthesis and information curation

- Structured note-taking techniques (Zettelkasten, PARA, Cornell method)
- Reference management tools (Zotero, Notion, Obsidian)
- Synthesizing multiple sources into actionable conclusions (not just "summarizing")
- Detecting contradictions between sources and how to resolve or present them

## Project requirements specifically

- Requirements elicitation techniques (interviews, surveys, stakeholder analysis)
- Competitive benchmarking and market analysis
- Analysis frameworks (SWOT, PESTEL, Porter's Five Forces depending on context)
- Documenting findings in usable formats (briefs, decision matrices, research maps)

## Critical thinking and epistemology

- Recognizing your own cognitive biases (confirmation, availability, anchoring)
- Handling uncertainty: communicate confidence levels, not just binary conclusions
- Distinguishing correlation from causation in data

## Ethics and legal

- Copyright and fair use when citing/reusing content
- Data privacy when researching people or companies (GDPR and regional equivalents)
- Transparency about the use of AI in the research process
