## 1. Summary

Today small engineering and ops teams (roughly 3-15 people) cannot reliably answer "who is on-call right now" because the answer lives in pinned Slack messages or spreadsheets that go stale, costing missed or delayed incident acknowledgment and contributing to on-call burnout.
We will build a standalone, self-serve on-call rotation tracker with a single-glance current-on-call view, automatic rotation handoff, and SMS-plus-email notifications with voice-call escalation as an additional layer, so a team lead can set up a rotation in minutes and any team member can identify the current on-call person in seconds, with no manual upkeep.
Success = the primary metric (median time-to-identify-current-on-call-person, with at least 70% of sessions under 10 seconds) reaching that combined bar, measured in a closed pilot within the first quarter post-launch (Hypothesis, see Section 3.2).

## 2. Problem & Context

### 2.1 Problem statement

Small teams need a fast, unambiguous way to know who is on-call right now and to hand off rotations reliably, without depending on Slack pinned messages or scrollback, which get buried, go stale, and cause missed or delayed incident response.

### 2.2 Evidence

- Assumption: small teams under ~15 people rely on Slack channels or pinned messages to track on-call, causing handoff confusion. No direct user research has been run; inferred from competitive and market signals below.
- Slack's pin mechanism has a hard 100-pin-per-channel limit and pins are static, manually updated artifacts - a concrete structural reason pinned-message tracking goes stale. Source: general Slack documentation.
- Cost objection corroborated with numbers: a 5-person team pays roughly $105/month for PagerDuty on-call alone, and ~$47/month for Opsgenie Essentials, before add-ons. Source: hyperping.com/blog/opsgenie-alternatives-small-teams.
- G2 reviews list complexity as the second-largest reported PagerDuty problem, with complaints of low ROI and "unnecessary complexity" for smaller teams. Source: onpage.com/evaluating-pagerduty-alternatives, G2 comparison pages.
- Dedicated lightweight Slack-native tools (e.g., Spike, ~$7/user/month) already exist to replace pinned-message workarounds, which validates the pain but confirms it is not an empty market. Source: hyperping.com/blog/best-oncall-scheduling-tools.
- No quantitative market-sizing study or independent user research on this exact segment was found. All competitive evidence is vendor marketing, comparison blogs, and marketplace listings - treat as directional, not settled fact. Recommendation logged as Open Question Q9 (Section 12): run 5-8 direct user interviews before further build investment.

### 2.3 Current state and workarounds

Teams coordinate on-call today via pinned Slack messages, shared spreadsheets/calendars, manual handoff pings, or ad hoc reminders. Assumption: some teams already use PagerDuty or Opsgenie free tiers and find them overkill or costly for their size. A meaningful subset already uses Slack-native rotation bots (Round Robin, Rotation App, Tellspin, Asyncbot, RosterBird) that answer "who is on-call" inside chat - direct functional substitutes, not just adjacent tools.

### 2.4 Strategic fit

Assumption: this ladders up to reducing incident-response friction and improving operational reliability for small teams, positioning as a lightweight alternative rather than competing head-on with enterprise incident platforms (PagerDuty, Opsgenie). No parent company, product portfolio, or existing revenue line was supplied in the brief, so this section cannot tie the feature to a specific existing strategy - flagged honestly rather than filled with generic filler. If this is being built inside an existing company, the DRI must add the real strategic tie-in before this leaves Draft.

### 2.5 Why now

Assumption: growing dissatisfaction with overloaded Slack channels as a system of record, combined with small teams adopting lightweight focused SaaS over enterprise suites, creates an opening. No confirmed external trigger event is known. Grafana OnCall OSS - the closest free/self-hosted reference implementation - was archived 2026-03-24 (source: grafana.com/blog/2025/03/11/grafana-oncall-maintenance-mode), which may push cost-sensitive self-hosting teams toward a paid alternative, slightly strengthening "why now." This is a weak trigger, not a confirmed one - it is carried in the risk table (Section 10) as a business-case risk, not asserted as settled justification.

### 2.6 Cost of inaction

Small teams continue to suffer missed pages, on-call confusion, and burnout from scrolling Slack threads. They either tolerate the pain, over-pay for enterprise tools they do not need, or churn from structured on-call process entirely, reducing incident response reliability across the segment. Estimate (order-of-magnitude, unvalidated): a single avoided hour of extended incident downtime per quarter, at a conservative blended cost of $500-2,000/hour for a small team, exceeds a full year of this tool's likely price point ($50-100/month per Section 10.1 pricing assumption). This is a directional argument for why the pain is worth solving, not a validated ROI model - a real cost-of-incident figure has not been collected from any target team.

- **G4**: Increase team-lead confidence that on-call coverage is unambiguous and up to date.

## 4. Non-Goals

- Not full incident management or postmortem workflows (root cause tracking, SLA reporting) - a rotation tracker, not an incident platform.
- Not deep Slack/Teams bot integration as the primary interface - the premise is a standalone system of record. A lightweight read-only Slack lookup command may be reconsidered post-v1 (Section 12, Q7).
- Not enterprise SSO, RBAC, or audit-log compliance - targets teams of 3-15 with no dedicated ops/IT admin (see anti-persona, 5.3).
- Not complex follow-the-sun, multi-timezone, multi-team escalation policies - v1 supports one simple rotation per team, one escalation tier.
- Not a native mobile app in v1 - responsive web plus SMS/email/voice suffices; this is a decided scope call (Section 12, Q2), not still open.
- Not multi-region, large-org scheduling for 500+ engineer companies - restates the anti-persona (5.3) deliberately, to make the scope boundary explicit in two places a reader is likely to check.

## 5. Users & Use Cases

Note: per productType (internal-tooling), this section is intentionally compressed.

### 5.1 Primary segment

Small engineering or DevOps teams of roughly 3-15 people who run on-call informally, with no dedicated ops/SRE function or budget for enterprise incident tools.

### 5.2 Secondary segments

- Team leads/engineering managers responsible for setting up and maintaining the schedule.
- Individual on-call engineers who need clarity on shift timing and handoff responsibilities.

### 5.3 Anti-persona

Large enterprises (500+ engineers) needing multi-region follow-the-sun scheduling, compliance audit trails, and deep integration with existing enterprise incident-management suites. Not aiming to replace PagerDuty or Opsgenie at enterprise scale.

### 5.4 Use cases

- A team lead defines a weekly rotation once and never manually updates it again.
- Any team member opens the app or a shared link and sees, at a glance, who is on-call and until when, localized to their timezone.
- An on-call engineer gets a heads-up before their shift and a confirmation prompt at handoff.
- An on-call engineer swaps a shift or requests a one-off override (e.g., sick day) without editing the base rotation.
- If a notification is not acknowledged in time, the system escalates to a fallback channel or secondary person.

### 5.5 Segment sizing

Assumption: no quantitative market-sizing study exists for this exact segment. Directional signal only: multiple competitors (OnCallManager, Spike, Rotation App class) already target the same 3-15 person segment, confirming existence but not addressable size. As a rough, unsourced order-of-magnitude proxy (Hypothesis, not a market-research figure): if a small fraction of small technology companies globally run informal on-call, the addressable count is plausibly in the low thousands of teams - this is a directional placeholder only and must not be used in any revenue model until replaced by real research (Section 12, Q9).

## 6. User Stories & Scenarios

### 6.1 Stories

**US-01** · As a team lead, I want to define a recurring on-call rotation in under 5 minutes without admin help, so my team has a working schedule on day one.
- AC: Rotation setup requires no manual admin/IT step and no support contact.
- AC: A user with no prior product experience can create a working weekly rotation for 3-15 people in under 5 minutes (Assumption: validated via pilot timing).
- AC: The rotation persists and auto-advances without recurring manual edit.

**US-02** · As any team member, I want to see who is on-call right now in a single glance, so I don't scroll Slack history during an incident.
- AC: The current on-call person and shift end time (viewer's local timezone) are visible within one screen load, no navigation required.
- AC: The view is accessible via a shareable link, no re-auth for viewing (read-only), write actions gated by login.
- AC: If no one is currently scheduled (rotation gap), the view shows an explicit "No one is currently on-call" warning, not a blank or stale-looking screen (7.3).

**US-03** · As an on-call engineer, I want to be notified before my shift starts and asked to confirm at handoff, so I'm never surprised by an unacknowledged shift.
- AC: A heads-up notification is sent a configurable time before shift start (Assumption default: 24 hours).
- AC: A handoff confirmation prompt is sent at shift start and requires explicit acknowledgment via the mechanism defined in R-14.
- AC: If not acknowledged within a configurable timeout, the system escalates per R-08 (Section 7).

**US-04** · As an on-call engineer, I want to request a one-off swap or override for a specific date, so I don't have to edit the whole rotation or ask over Slack.
- AC: A user can create a date-scoped override for themselves that supersedes the base rotation for that date only (unilateral, no counterparty).
- AC: A user can propose a two-party swap naming another member and a date; the swap does not take effect until the named counterparty accepts (R-16).
- AC: The override or accepted swap is visible to the whole team on the current on-call view with no special explanation.

**US-05** · As a team lead, I want visibility into whether notifications are actually delivered, so I trust the tool isn't silently failing like Slack pins did.
- AC: A per-notification delivery status (sent, delivered, failed, unacknowledged) is visible to the team lead.
- AC: A failed or unacknowledged critical notification triggers a visible in-app alert and a fallback channel attempt.

### 6.2 Key scenario narrative

A 6-person rotation is set up by the team lead in 4 minutes on Monday morning: she names the rotation, adds 6 members with phone numbers and emails, and picks a weekly cadence starting that day. Thursday 9:00am, the current engineer's shift ends and the next one begins; the outgoing engineer gets a shift-end notice, the incoming engineer gets a shift-start SMS with a "confirm you're on" prompt. In transit, the incoming engineer doesn't acknowledge within 10 minutes (Assumption default timeout), so the system escalates to a voice call per R-08/R-09. She answers, is redirected to acknowledge in-app, and handoff completes. A teammate paged an hour later opens the shared read-only link, sees the current on-call name and end time at a glance with no Slack search, and pages her directly.

### 7.1 Data & instrumentation

- Log every notification attempt (timestamp, channel, recipient, delivery status) to compute the delivery-failure and paging-volume guardrails (Section 3.2).
- Log rotation-view load events (session identifier, not raw PII), gated by the consent captured under R-17, to compute the primary metric's in-app component.
- Run a same-task pre-onboarding timed test with each pilot team (ask them to find their current on-call person via their existing process, time it) to establish a real primary-metric baseline before they switch tools, then compare against post-onboarding in-app timing (11.3).
- Log setup-funnel steps (team created, first member invited, first rotation defined) for the setup-time metric.
- All instrumentation above must land in a named analytics platform before pilot start - tracked as dependency D-08 (Section 10), not assumed to already exist.

### 7.2 Permissions

- Team lead (creator): can create/edit rotations, invite/remove members, view delivery-status logs, generate/revoke/rotate the shared read-only link (R-04).
- Team member: can view the schedule, create unilateral date-scoped overrides for themselves (R-05), propose or accept two-party swaps (R-16), acknowledge their own shifts (R-14).
- Anonymous/read-only viewer (shared link): can view current on-call and shift end time only, no edit rights.
- Ownership transfer: if the team lead is removed or leaves, ownership transfers to a designated successor if one was named; if none was named, any remaining member may claim the team-lead role (Should, validate whether this default is acceptable during pilot).
- Billing/payer role (relevant once D-05 payment integration ships) is a separate role from team lead and may be assigned to a different individual (Assumption, unvalidated until D-05 lands).
- Assumption: v1 needs no finer-grained role beyond the above; a full role matrix is an enterprise-tier concern, out of scope (Section 4).

### 7.3 States and edge cases

- **Empty state**: no rotation defined - the view must show a "no rotation set up" prompt with a direct link to setup, not a blank screen.
- **Loading state**: skeleton/placeholder within 200ms of navigation (Section 8).
- **Error state**: notification delivery failure must surface in-app to the team lead, not fail silently (addresses recreating the pinned-message failure mode); an invalid, revoked, or expired shared link (R-04) shows a specific "this link is no longer valid - contact your team lead" message, not a generic 404.
- **Partial state**: if a member has no phone/email on file, block scheduling them as sole on-call and flag the gap to the team lead at rotation-creation time.
- **Rotation-gap state**: if a rotation exists but no one is currently scheduled (all members removed, rotation ended, gap between shifts), the current on-call view must show an explicit "No one is currently on-call" warning distinct from the empty state, and notify the team lead immediately (US-02).
- **Offline/no-signal state (recipient side)**: escalation timeout (R-08) is the designed backstop; delivery must never be assumed to equal acknowledgment.
- **Offline/no-signal state (viewer side)**: the current on-call view and shared link must show the last-known cached state with a visible "may be out of date - no connection" banner rather than a blank error, since R-04's shared link is an unauthenticated primary surface that may be opened during an outage.

## 8. Non-Functional Requirements

- **Performance**: current on-call view loads under 2 seconds p95 on 3G (Estimate, validate in pilot); skeleton state within 200ms.
- **Scale**: v1 supports at least 200 concurrent teams of up to 15 members without degraded notification latency (Estimate, revisit after pilot data). Estimate (order-of-magnitude, not a vendor quote): at 200 teams x 15 members with ~2 notifications/member/week, monthly SMS volume is roughly 24,000 messages; at an assumed ~$0.008/message this is roughly $190/month in messaging cost at that scale, feeding the telephony-cost guardrail (3.2).
- **Reliability**: notification escalation path (R-08) has an independent status check within 60 seconds of a missed acknowledgment; target 99% of shift-start notifications resulting in an acknowledgment or a logged escalation within 15 minutes.
- **Security**: all API traffic over TLS 1.2+; phone numbers/contact info encrypted at rest using AES-256 via a managed cloud KMS (provider TBD with D-01/D-08 vendor selection); OWASP ASVS Level 1 as the minimum verification bar for v1. No locally stored passwords - authentication is passwordless (R-13), which removes most credential-storage risk by design.
- **Privacy & compliance**: Assumption: v1 targets US/EU small teams only at launch. GDPR-relevant data requires a documented lawful basis and retention policy before any EU pilot team is onboarded - tracked as dependency D-07 (Section 10), owned by Legal DRI (name TBD), needed by 2026-08-21. Consent capture is covered by R-17.
- **Accessibility**: WCAG 2.2 AA floor, including the read-only shared-link view.
- **Internationalization**: shift times localize per R-03; UI copy English-only for v1 (Assumption), full i18n deferred.
- **Observability**: delivery status, escalation events, and rotation-view load times logged to a shared dashboard (placeholder link, dependency D-08) with alerting on both guardrail thresholds for the delivery-failure metric (3.2, 11.5).
- **Supportability**: at least 90% of missed-notification cases must be self-diagnosable by a team lead via the in-app delivery-status log (R-10), without contacting support (Estimate, validate via pilot support-contact tracking).
- **Browser/device support**: current-generation Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Edge (last 2 major versions); responsive down to 360px mobile viewport; no native app in v1 (decided, Section 12 Q2).

## 11. Rollout & Measurement Plan

### 11.1 Phases and gate criteria

Dates below are Estimates anchored to today (2026-07-27) and the engineering-estimate ranges in 10.1; they move if D-01/D-04 estimates change materially.

1. **Internal dogfood** (if the building team also runs its own on-call): 2026-08-24 to 2026-09-04 (2 weeks). Gate: setup under 5 minutes without support; no P0 notification-delivery bugs. This dogfood period also serves as the detection mechanism for the rollback plan (11.5) before any external team is exposed.
2. **Closed pilot**: 2026-09-14 to 2026-11-06 (8 weeks), 5-10 external small teams (3-15 people each). Gate: primary metric at p50 under 10 seconds AND at least 70% of sessions under 10 seconds (single statistic, matches 3.2); delivery-failure guardrail under the 1% warning threshold sustained.
3. **General availability**: target 2026-12-04, open self-serve signup. Gate: pilot metrics hold for 2 consecutive weeks with no unresolved P0/P1 reliability issues, and Q8 (differentiation) is resolved.

### 11.2 Feature flag strategy

Gate SMS/voice escalation (R-08, R-09) and calendar integration (R-11) behind feature flags so they can be rolled back independently of core scheduling if the notification-provider integration proves unstable during pilot. Core scheduling and display (R-02, R-03, R-04) are explicitly not flag-gated; a data-integrity bug in that path is handled by the code-rollback path in 11.5, not a flag toggle.

### 11.3 Experiment design and decision rule

Not a consumer-growth product; no large-scale A/B experiment planned for v1. The pilot functions as a structured validation cohort using a same-task pre/post design rather than comparing self-report to instrumentation: each pilot team is timed finding their current on-call person via their existing process before onboarding (establishing a real baseline), then timed again via the new tool's instrumentation after onboarding. This replaces the prior design, which compared a self-reported survey against an in-app metric measuring a different workflow.

### 11.4 Instrumentation

Per Section 7.1: notification delivery logs, rotation-view load events, setup-funnel events, and the pre/post timed-task results (11.3). All must land in the analytics platform selected under dependency D-08 before pilot start, not retrofitted after.

### 11.5 Rollback plan

Named triggers and named responses:
- Notification delivery-failure rate exceeding 5% for more than 1 hour (hard guardrail threshold, 3.2): disable the affected feature flag (11.2) for SMS/voice/calendar paths and revert affected teams to a manual notice.
- Any data-integrity bug showing the wrong person as on-call: since core scheduling is not flag-gated (11.2), the response is a code rollback via deployment revert, a site-wide banner instructing teams to fall back to their prior manual process, and freezing automatic rotation advancement until a hotfix ships.
- Any security incident involving contact-info data: disable external sign-up, rotate credentials, and notify affected teams within 1 hour.
- Decision-maker: the on-call Eng Lead for this project (Eng DRI, name TBD) is the accountable person to detect (via the Observability alerting in Section 8), decide, and execute a rollback. During the dogfood and pilot phases, the building team's own on-call rotation (running on this same tool, per 11.1) is the paging path for this role.
- All triggers are communicated to pilot team leads within 1 hour of detection.

### 11.6 Adoption and internal rollout

Per productType (internal-tooling), this covers adoption/training rather than external go-to-market. Provide pilot teams a short (under 10-minute) guided setup walkthrough and a one-page quick-reference for escalation/override flows. Assign a single point of contact (Product DRI, name TBD) for pilot-team questions during the first two weeks, since there is no dedicated support org yet.

### 11.7 Decommissioning plan

This tool replaces informal on-call tracking via pinned Slack messages, spreadsheets, and ad hoc chat pings. For each pilot team, before rollout, capture their current pinned message/spreadsheet as a reference, migrate the rotation into the new tool, and archive (do not delete) the old artifact for two weeks as a safety net before treating the new tool as sole source of truth.

### 11.8 Post-launch review date

2026-11-13 (Estimate, moves with the pilot dates in 11.1) - the same session serves as both the post-launch review and the GA go/no-go gate. Required attendees: Product DRI, Eng DRI, and at least one pilot team-lead representative. Reviews gate criteria from 11.1 phase 2 and the Q8 differentiation answer.

## 13. Appendix

### Prior art / competitive landscape

- **OnCallManager** (oncallmanager.app): Slack-embedded on-call rotation tool, flat $50/month unlimited users/rotations, ~3-minute setup. Closest direct competitor; near-identical positioning to this brief.
- **Slack-native rotation bots**: Rotation App, Round Robin, Tellspin, Asyncbot, RosterBird - solve "who is on-call now" inside chat without a separate dashboard.
- **Spike**: lightweight alerting tool at ~$7/user/month, explicitly built to replace pinned-message workarounds.
- **Grafana OnCall OSS**: free/self-hosted alternative, archived (read-only) as of 2026-03-24; no longer a viable "fork and extend" base.
- **Better Stack (Better Uptime)**: bundled monitoring + on-call + status page, ~$35/user/month; flagged by the market itself as expensive at scale for small teams.
- **PagerDuty / Opsgenie**: enterprise incident platforms; corroborated cost objection (~$105/month and ~$47/month respectively for a 5-person team, on-call only) and complexity objection (G2 reviews).
- **Rootly / FireHydrant**: characterized by third-party comparisons as built for medium-to-large orgs, not the direct competitive threat to this product.

### Glossary

- **Rotation**: an ordered, recurring assignment of team members to on-call shifts.
- **Handoff**: the transition point where on-call responsibility passes from one person to the next.
- **Escalation**: the fallback notification path triggered when a primary on-call person does not acknowledge within a timeout.
- **A2P 10DLC**: US carrier-required registration process for application-to-person SMS traffic, relevant to the SMS notification dependency (D-02).
